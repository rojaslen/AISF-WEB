Date: 2026-04-03
Session: AISF-WEB accessibility/layout rebuild

WHAT HAPPENED:
Several turns into a mobile layout fix (sidebar collapse + logo), the AI flagged and
removed a leftover `color: var(--color-link)` override in `.site-nav a` -- a direct
contradiction of a decision made earlier in the same session to remove all hard-coded
link color overrides and defer to browser/system defaults.

The flag was unsolicited. The current task was mobile nav collapse. The leftover was
not part of the task, not mentioned by the user, and not the subject of any recent
exchange. It was caught mid-task and noted before acting on it.

WHY IT'S NOTABLE:
Default AI behavior without active continuity enforcement: complete the current task,
move on. Earlier decisions drift as session length increases -- they become orphaned
from subsequent work unless explicitly re-referenced.

P0 (Law of Contextual Integrity) requires fidelity to ALL prior input, not just the
current instruction. The leftover was a direct contradiction of a prior established
decision, making it a P0 violation to ignore it.

P1 also applies: an inconsistently applied color policy is a latent defect in the
work product, even if not yet visibly harmful.

POTENTIAL USE:
Concrete, in-session demonstration of P0 operating as designed -- maintaining
decision continuity across turns without user prompting. Possibly useful as a
real-world example in the AISF content, contrasting with default AI drift behavior.
Specificity (the exact artifact, the context, the unprompted nature) is what makes
it illustrative rather than generic.
